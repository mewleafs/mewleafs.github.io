# Leafs Live

A desktop app for Windows for live streamers. It reads your Twitch and YouTube chats in one place,
with 7TV, BetterTTV and FrankerFaceZ emotes drawn like Twitch's own (everyone's and your channel's,
with an emote picker in the message box), lets you answer from either account, lets you delete messages and time out, ban or unban viewers
from the same feed, lets you allow or deny the messages Twitch's AutoMod holds, keeps a log of
every moderation action in your chats with an undo for timeouts and bans, runs chat commands typed in the message box (`/ban`, `/slow`, `/raid`…), shows
each chat's modes (slow mode, followers-only, members-only…) with a switch for Twitch's and a raid
shield button that turns on the protections you picked and back off, and drives customizable OBS
overlays (chat, featured message, alerts, chat race, poll, giveaway, credits, now playing, Word Sprout, word brawl) and an OBS dock
from a server that runs only on your PC. Simple rules can hide, dim, highlight or tag messages,
and, once you turn them on, delete messages or time out or warn their authors (blocked words,
links, shouting in capitals, too many emotes, the same text again), never touching you, your
moderators or your bots. Reader mode shows the chat in pages, in the app and the dock, so you
read every message in order: a page moves on only when you press Next, it counts what you haven't
read yet, and it skips the messages your rules dim and the names you tell it to skip (such as bots)
but never a follow, sub, raid or other alert. Important mode pages the same way through only
what mustn't be missed (follows, subs, cheers, raids, Super Chats, memberships, hype trains,
first-time chatters and the channel points rewards you pick), with its own count always in view
in the app and the dock. Alerts thank viewers in chat when they follow, subscribe, gift subs,
cheer, raid, redeem a channel points reward or use a Power-up on Twitch, cheer on Twitch hype
trains as they start, level up and end, and thank viewers who send a Super Chat, a Super Sticker,
a Jewels gift, become a member or gift memberships on YouTube: each type is off until you turn it
on, with your own messages and variables, amount tiers, a minimum, rewards picked from your
channel's list with messages of their own, and one line for a burst of them, sent as your bot
account when you have one. Viewer commands answer what viewers type in either chat (`!discord`,
`!uptime`, `!followage`, `!lurk`, `!so @friend`, `!8ball`, a `!deaths` counter…) in that chat, once
you turn them on: every command can be edited, turned off or deleted, with your own replies and
variables, who can use it, cooldowns and a gentler pace on YouTube to spare its daily quota, and
you can add your own. Timed messages post your lines (your Discord, your socials, a reminder of
the commands…) in each of your live chats every few minutes, taking turns in your order, once you
turn them on, and only after enough viewers have written since the last one, so a quiet chat isn't
spammed. The same alerts play on stream in an Alerts widget, one at a time, with your own
picture, GIF or video and sound from your PC, set for each type (and each amount tier or
channel points reward) once for every overlay, in the Alerts view or the overlay editor, and each
overlay's own text and look, and you can skip or replay them from the app or the dock. Sounds play about as loud as each other, however loud their
files are: the app measures each one once and evens them out, with your volumes on top. A chat race shows who got to your stream first: each viewer's first message, follow,
sub, redeem or other support takes the next place, gold, silver and bronze first, on stream as a
leaderboard, a ticker or one arrival at a time, with an all-time board of who came first most
often; it starts with each stream (or when you start it), leaves out your own accounts and known
bots, and lists everyone in the app and the dock with a *Said hi* tick, so you can greet each one
while getting ready. Polls run in both chats at once: one choice, several choices or an open
answer, voted by typing an option's number or text (or `!vote`), one vote per account on each
platform, with a time or until you end it, who can vote, the result live on stream or only at the
end (Twitch and YouTube shown apart if you like) and, if you choose, a line in chat when it starts
and ends, in your language, with the answers in quotes (on Twitch only, if you'd rather not spend
YouTube's quota); you make, start, end or cancel them from the app or the dock, save polls to run again
and see past ones with their results in the app. Giveaways also run in both chats at once: viewers enter with a
keyword like `!join`, a name that matches a pattern (such as a Guild Wars 2 account name, kept as
their entry) or any message, one entry per account, and a name counts once; subscribers and
members get more chances (×2 unless you change it), and you can limit it to some platforms, to
subscribers or higher, to Twitch followers or Twitch accounts of a certain age, and leave
moderators out. You make and start giveaways from the app or the dock, close the entries (or they
close at their time), draw a winner weighted by those chances, reroll, copy the winner's name or
entry (a Guild Wars 2 account name to send the prize to) with one click, and can ask the winner to answer in chat within a time; the giveaway shows
on stream with its prize, how to enter, the time left, a shuffle of names and the winner, and,
if you choose, lines in chat when it opens, before it closes and for the winner once the shuffle on stream ends. Unless you turn
it off, each viewer who enters is told in their chat that they're in, and one who tries again that
they're in already (once), with several at a time sharing one line. Past giveaways
keep their winners and a draw log with each draw's seed and entries, so a draw can be checked.
Word Sprout, a word game for the whole chat, runs in both chats at once: after a short countdown
(the letters hidden until it ends), a round shows scrambled letters and an empty slot for every
word they make (English or Spanish), viewers type the words they find, and the first to find one
fills its slot with their name for everyone and scores its length, the word that uses every letter
twice. When the time runs out, guesses still count for a few seconds to make up for the stream's
delay. Reaching the round's goal passes the level and gives everyone who found a word a bonus;
missing it ends the game, or with Endless on starts again at level 1. Rounds follow one another,
with pause, skip, hints and a shuffle from the app or the dock, and boards of this stream's and
all-time points (accounts you linked as one person count together). It shows on stream as its own widget, and the Chat widget can
leave the guesses out. Nothing about it is posted to chat.
The word brawl is a co-op typing fight for both chats at once, face to face like an old RPG: after
a short countdown, one pixel-art enemy at a time faces the stream with a few words under it, and
chat types the words to hit it, one hit per viewer per word, with everyone sharing its health; each
hit makes its word glow and the viewer's name fly into the enemy. Enemies are Common, Rare, Epic or
Legendary, which sets how many words they show, how long the words are, how much health they have
and how often they cast. Many come with gimmicks, each shown on stream so chat knows what to do: a
typo to fix or to copy as it is, words drawn backwards, scrambled, blotted out or glitching, armored
words several viewers break together, a decoy word that heals the enemy, enemies that heal when left
alone, words that split in two or change faster, enemies that add a word when badly hurt, and enemies
that grab the top hitter, whose hits heal them until chat frees them with a rescue word; you
can turn any of them off. A fight is a list of waves you pick (a rarity or a specific enemy, as many
times as you like), with Easy, Normal and Hard ready-made and your own saved under a name, each
keeping its best time. A word that changed
stays hittable for a few seconds, and so do a fallen enemy's, for the stream's delay. When the
enemy casts, enough different viewers typing its cast word interrupt it, or the fight's time takes
a penalty that the clock shows. There's no losing, only a time to beat for each saved fight, shown
by the clock, and the result shows the time, what the penalties added and the top three by hits;
boards keep each fight's, this stream's and all-time hits. Raids, subs, bits, Super Chats and
Power-ups can add more enemies of the rarity you pick to the end of the fight if you turn that on. The enemies are
isaiah658's free pixel-art monsters, each with its name on stream; you can remove the ones you don't want and add
your own: a picture from your library or a Twitch viewer's profile picture, a rarity, the gimmicks you choose,
their own words mixed in with the normal ones and lines they say in a speech bubble when they come in, cast and
are beaten. You can also make a viewer the boss right there, from their viewer card or by name, with their
name and profile picture: everyone else fights them as always, while only they get boss words, one after another, that charge a heal, a gimmick
for a while or time added to the clock (how many words and how strong is yours to set, mild by default),
and what they write shows as the boss talking. Or you can give the monster on screen a viewer's voice: they still
play, and their other messages show in its speech bubble. Only messages that would show on stream appear there,
and one a moderator deletes leaves it. It shows on stream as its own
widget, with sounds you pick from your library, and the Chat widget can leave the typed words out.
The Chat widget can also step off the stream while either game runs and come back after it.
Either game can take only messages starting with ! as plays (viewer commands stay commands), and
says so on stream, and the plays in the dock's and the app's chat can show, fold into one line per
run, or stay out.
Nothing about it is posted to chat.
Credits roll at the end of a stream with everyone who supports you, or only who supported that
stream: Twitch subscribers (everyone subscribed now, not gifted subs), gifters with their totals,
the month's bits (with Twitch's leaderboard of the month), YouTube members of the last month, gifted memberships,
Super Chats and Stickers (names only) and Jewels, the stream's hype train top contributors and
raids, and if you like the stream's moderators and top chatters; subscribers and members can be
grouped by their Twitch tier or YouTube membership level; each section can be turned off, renamed and reordered, with your own
title and end text, a background, a speed or a total time, and a loop. You roll them from the app, the dock or
the overlay editor; they never include follows.
Stream ideas keep a list of things to do on stream (ideas, topics, things to
react to or read), each with a note, a kind, links to open or copy and an optional day from which
it's important: important ones come first, and the dock's button counts them with a small bounce
when one comes due; search, filter by kind, drag to reorder, tick them done. They're in the app and
the dock, never on stream. Stream info sets your stream's details on both platforms from one
form, in the app and the dock: a title shared by both or one for each, Twitch's category (search
by name), tags, language, content classification labels and branded content, and the YouTube
broadcast's description, category, visibility, made for kids and a thumbnail from your pictures,
for the live broadcast, an upcoming one or a new one you schedule on a stream key; save it as a
preset ("Guild Wars 2 night") to apply again with one click. Discord notifications, off until
you set one up, announce each stream in your Discord server through a webhook: one message per
stream with the roles you pick mentioned where you put them, written in a visual editor or as
Discord's JSON (with variables for the title, category, viewers, links, how long you've been live
and more, and a preview in Discord's look), the stream's picture, updated every 30 minutes or as
often as you choose while you're live, and edited, deleted, left or replaced by your ended message
when the stream ends; several webhooks can post to several servers, and *Send a test* tries one.
Now playing shows the song playing on your PC (Spotify, a browser, Apple Music or any app in
Windows' media controls, with no login) on stream with its cover and a moving progress bar, and in
the app and the dock with Previous, Play or Pause and Next for that app; it follows Windows'
current app or only the apps you pick, so a video in the browser doesn't take over from your music.
`!song` and the `{song}` variable say it in chat, and a line on each new track can too, once you
turn it on.
Channel points manages your Twitch
rewards in the app and the dock: make one with its cost, prompt, color, limits per stream and per
viewer, cooldown and whether it skips the queue, then pause it, turn it off, edit it or delete it;
what viewers redeem waits in a queue to complete or refund, one at a time or all of a reward at
once. Rewards made on twitch.tv are listed too, and can be recreated in Leafs Live to manage them
from there. Each reward also says how its redemptions show in the chat: grouped in one row that
counts them and who redeemed them (so a spammed reward doesn't bury the messages), a row each so
what viewers typed can be read, or not in the chat at all. VIP Stamps, off until you turn it on,
is a passport on Twitch: viewers get one stamp per stream by redeeming the reward you pick, and
those who reach your amount in a month are made Twitch VIPs for the next month, which they keep by
reaching it again; it never touches the VIPs you gave on twitch.tv, never makes a moderator a VIP,
and permanent VIPs you choose never lose it. The month change happens by itself, with a report of
who gained and lost VIP and why, a vacation mode where nobody loses it, an increase that starts
next month and an optional line in chat naming the new VIPs. Each stamp shows on stream with this
stream's icon stamped in by a rubber stamp, in the look you pick: a slim ticket with the month's stamps in a row, a
ticket where they're stops on a route to VIP, or the viewer's open passport, at the size each
overlay sets. Reaching the amount plays a celebration, the first activity in a stream of a VIP you
gave a sound of their own plays a welcome (on screen, or only its sound) with that sound at a
volume of its own and sends a welcome line (every VIP gets one if you choose), and `!passport` (off
until you turn it on) tells a viewer how many stamps they have. Viewers lists everyone your chats
have seen on Twitch and YouTube, searchable and sorted by when they last or first came, streams,
messages or support: when each was last seen and in which stream, how many of the latest streams
they came to and streams in a row, who only watched on Twitch, their messages, when they usually
arrive, their first message, what they gave (Super Chats in their own currency), their channel
points, chat race places, polls and giveaways, with filters for this stream, new viewers,
regulars, regulars who've gone missing and supporters, and a summary of each stream (chatters,
new and returning viewers, messages per minute). Each stream has a recap: its numbers next to the
average of your last streams, messages per minute on both platforms or each apart with the viewer
counts, the busiest moments and what happened then (a raid, a burst of alerts, a marker) with the
chat of that moment, the top chatters, the regulars who came and the ones who didn't, what came in
(follows, subs and gift subs by tier, bits, memberships, Super Chats per currency, Jewels, raids in
and out, channel points), the chat race's podium, polls, giveaways, ad breaks and markers. A banner
offers it when a stream ends; export it as CSV, JSON or a picture to share, or post it to Discord.
Hype moments are found while you're live: both chats rising against their own pace (laughter, hype,
shock, a message many copy), hype trains, raids, gift bursts, big cheers, Super Chats close together,
viewers' clips and viewer count jumps, as one moment when both platforms rise together, or a moment
you mark yourself. Each is placed a little before the chat reacted, with its time on Twitch and on
YouTube and a link to the stream at that point, gets a private Twitch stream marker unless you turn
that off, and waits in the app and the dock to keep or dismiss. The recap lists them with the chat
of that moment, makes a Twitch clip of one when you press Clip it, and writes them as chapters in
the YouTube video's description when you press Add chapters, so the moments stay findable after
Twitch's video is gone. Nothing about them is shown on stream.
When a stream ends, Leafs Live writes its stream pack into the profile folder for Leafs Content
(Leafs' app for making videos) to find shorts with: when it was live on each platform and its
videos, the hype moments with their chat as text only, messages per minute, markers, ad breaks and
viewers' Twitch clips, including the ones made after the stream. The two apps never need to be open
together; YouTube's parts leave the file after 30 days.
While you're live, a strip under the header of the app and the dock shows the stream's numbers:
viewers on both platforms added up with each one's and a trend arrow, the peak, how long you've
been live, who's in the Twitch chat, messages per minute, chatters and new chatters, follows, subs
and gift subs, bits, the hype train's level, the next ad, YouTube's likes and views (once you turn
YouTube stats on), memberships and Super Chats per currency. A click hides a stat, + brings it
back, and the strip folds to one button; nothing of it is shown on stream.
The same stats are on each viewer's card in the
app and the dock, where you can link a person's Twitch and YouTube accounts as one, and a regular's
first message after missing streams is marked Welcome back (rules and important mode can use it). Moderation shows your Twitch channel's moderation settings in the app and the dock:
the unban requests banned viewers send, to approve or deny with a note, counted on the dock's
button; AutoMod's level, one for everything or one per category; and Twitch's blocked terms next
to Leafs Live's own blocked words, to add, remove and copy from one list to the other. Twitch
ads show in the Stream view, the app's header and the dock: when the next ad break comes, how
long it is and the snoozes left, with Snooze and Run an ad. A heads-up shortly before an ad and
while one runs offers Pause alerts and Tell chat, and alerts can pause by themselves for each ad
break and play what waited after it. Lines you turn on tell the Twitch chat by themselves before a
scheduled ad, when a break starts and when it ends. One Play /
Pause / Stop control, in the app's header and the dock's, holds or drops
everything that can interrupt the stream: alerts on stream, featured messages and, if you choose,
the thank-you messages and the channel points rewards Leafs Live's Twitch app created, which it
pauses on Twitch itself. A simulator makes fake chat messages, follows, subs, raids, Super Chats, bans,
AutoMod holds and more, one at a time or as saved sequences, so you can try your alerts, overlays
and rules without a stream: they happen in simulated chats, nothing of them is saved, and the lines
they would send to chat are only shown unless you choose to really send them. Remote control, off
until you turn it on, lets a Stream Deck, Bitfocus Companion, AutoHotkey or a script work the
stream by calling a URL on your PC with a key only you have: play, pause, stop, skip or replay
alerts; turn reader and important mode's pages and switch between the live chat, reader mode and
important mode; take the featured message off; start and end the stream session and the chat race;
start, end and show saved polls; open, close, draw and finish saved giveaways; start, pause,
resume, skip, hint, shuffle and stop Word Sprout and turn its Endless on and off; start, pause, resume and stop a word brawl fight; roll and stop the credits; snooze or run Twitch ads; play, pause or skip what's playing; turn Twitch's slow, emote-only, followers-only, subscribers-only
and Shield modes on and off and put a stream marker; apply saved stream info; send a line to chat;
turn timed messages and viewer commands on and off; and play simulator presets. Settings lists
every action with its URL ready to copy and a manual to save. The interface works in English and Spanish.

Everything runs on your streaming PC. There's no Leafs account and no Leafs server: the app talks
only to Twitch and YouTube for the accounts you connect, to Discord for the notifications you set
up, and to Google Fonts when you add a font.

## Google and YouTube

Connecting YouTube is optional. If you connect it, Leafs Live asks Google for `youtube.readonly`
to find your live streams and read their chat. Only if you choose to send messages to YouTube chat
(*Allow sending*, or a bot account) does it also ask for `youtube.force-ssl`, which it uses only to
post the messages you write, the thank-you messages you turn on, the answers to the viewer
commands you turn on and the timed messages you turn on, and to carry out the moderation you click or type in your own chat
(deleting a message, timing out, banning or unbanning a viewer, adding or removing a chat
moderator) or that a moderation rule you turned on does (deleting a message, timing out its
author). It never uploads or deletes videos, and it moderates only when you do or one of
your rules does. With the same permission it changes your live or upcoming broadcast's title,
description, category, visibility, audience setting and thumbnail, and schedules a new broadcast,
only when you press *Update the stream* or apply a stream info preset. Details are in the
[privacy policy](privacy/).
